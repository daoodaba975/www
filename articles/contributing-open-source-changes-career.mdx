# Contributing to Open Source Changes a Career

<MdxImage src="/blog/cover04.webp" alt="Cover" width={960} height={540} />

Contributing to open source is not just about writing code in public.  
It’s something that can transform the way you learn, collaborate… and sometimes your entire path as a developer.

<br />

This year, while preparing <MdxLink href="https://hacktoberfest.com" name="Hacktoberfest" /> with the <MdxLink href="https://galsen.dev" name="Galsen DEV" /> community, I had the chance to guide dozens of developers through their very first contributions.  
And what I observed reminded me of how powerful open source can be as a growth engine.

<br />
Here are five reasons why.

## Forces you to learn better and faster

When you contribute to an open source project, you’re exposed to real code.  
Not tutorial code — actual, production-level code written by experienced developers: structured, tested, documented.

<br />
You discover:

<MdxList
  items={[
    {
      label:
        "- real-world patterns and conventions that no course ever teaches,",
    },
    { label: "- architecture decisions explained through discussions," },
    {
      label:
        "- and sometimes… a bit of chaos too (which is part of the learning process).",
    },
  ]}
/>

<br />
Every PR and every review becomes an opportunity to learn something you would have
never discovered alone.

## You learn how to collaborate (for real)

You can be “good” while coding alone.  
But in open source, you become better by working with others.

<br />
You discuss ideas, explain your reasoning, learn clarity, diplomacy, precision and
you receive feedback that pushes you forward.

<br />

During Hacktoberfest, I saw developers ask questions on issues and solve real problems,
especially on the <MdxLink href="https://github.com/GalsenDev221/map" name="GalsenDev221/map" /> project, an open-source initiative to map and
connect the Senegalese tech community

## You help others (and that feeling is priceless)

One day, you merge a PR.  
A few weeks later, someone tells you: "Thank you, your contribution helped me."

<br />
That’s the moment you understand what open source really is : building something
bigger than yourself, together. It creates a sense of impact that few personal projects
can offer.

## Gives you visibility and opportunities

Recruiters, CTOs, lead developers, they all look at : your PRs, your repos, your ability to collaborate and your consistency over time.

<br />
Where a resume says "I know React", your GitHub shows how you use it, and how you
solve real problems.

## What I learned while guiding developers this year

As a lead/organizer within the Galsen DEV community, I realized that:

  items={[
    {

    },
    { label: "- Clear issues change everything." },
    {
      label: "- Good documentation is worth 10 hours of training.",

      label:

## Conclusion

Contributing to open source is not reserved for an elite.  
It’s accessible, human, educational, and deeply rewarding.

<br />
You don’t need to create the next React to bring value. A small fix, a clarified
README, or a simple improvement can help dozens of people.

<br />
And it might be the beginning of a new chapter in your career 🚀
